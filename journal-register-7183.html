The Le Jour Register: help us find the 7183.

Our own archive has gaps. A 1980 advertisement shows two chronographs side by side: the 7203, which collectors know well, and the 7183, which almost nobody has seen. If one is in your drawer, we would like to photograph it.

An advertisement from around 1980 shows three Le Jour chronographs under one line: «Self-winding chronographs, masterfully designed by Le Jour». Two references are named, No. 7203 and No. 7183. Both are listed at $300.

The 7203 is well documented. A dealer catalogue from June 1980 lists it next to a chronograph from one of the great houses, in the same case, with the same movement. Collectors know it, writers have reviewed it, and it is the reason the Corsa Master exists.

The 7183 is another story. We have seen it in that advertisement and almost nowhere else.

What we are looking for

If a Le Jour made before 1985 is in your drawer, we would like to photograph it. The 7183 most of all, but every reference helps: the 7203, the Ref. 12, the Valjoux 7733 and 7765 chronographs, the Yachtingraf regatta timers.

The reference from the case back or the papers. The calibre, if you have had it opened. The serial number. Photos of the dial, the case back and the movement. And the story: who bought it, where, and when.

We publish what we can verify and say what we can't.

What happens with your entry

Every entry in the Register is credited to its owner, by name or initials. We publish what we can verify and say what we can't. Contributors get first access to the Édition 1980.

Why a register

Le Jour's history has been told in several versions. We would rather tell one we can prove, and the best proof is the watches themselves.
