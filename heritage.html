00 · Heritage

The other name on the same chronograph.

Le Jour means the day. For two decades it was the name insiders wore. Here is what we know, and how we know it.

1960s05
1960s

05 · 1960s

A French house called The Day.

Collectors date the first Le Jour watches to the 1960s. A French house sold chronographs and divers under its own name, and brought Yema watches to America. Some of the Yachtingraf regatta timers sold there carry the Le Jour name on the dial.

1970s

10 · 1970s

Valjoux inside.

Hand-wound Valjoux 7733, 7734 and 7765 calibres. Reverse panda dials. A tachymeter at the edge, a telemeter scale inside, one red seconds hand. Some dials read France below the six. We print the same kind of line today.

The scales of a 1970s Le Jour chronograph, redrawn: tachymeter outside, telemeter inside.

1980

15 · 1980

Same case. Same movement.

A dealer catalogue lists the Le Jour 7203 at $300 and the Heuer Pasadena, reference 750.501, at $354. Heuer built both. OnTheDash, the reference work on Heuer chronographs, calls them the exact same watch. Collectors still call it the poor man's Heuer. We think its buyers were simply better informed.

1980 · No. 72032026 · Corsa Master

Source · onthedash.chronocentric.com · Dealer catalogue June 1980

1980s

20 · 1980s

The quiet years.

Quartz arrived and the name went quiet. The rights changed hands more than once.

2015

25 · 2015

Back on the dial.

The name returned with mechanical watches built around Sellita movements. First the Mark I chronograph, then divers, a GMT, the Corsa Master and the Rally Monte-Carlo.

Today

30 · Today

For those who know.

In June 1980, a dealer catalogue printed two chronographs on the same page.

Same case. Same movement.

One carried a famous name and cost $354.

The other said Le Jour and cost $300.

The people who chose Le Jour knew exactly what they were buying.

Today we make watches for the same kind of person.

For the pass road before sunrise and the start line of a regatta.

For the meeting that ends on time and the dinner that doesn't.

For the day, not the display case.

We name every movement.

We print the truth at six o'clock, where watchmakers always have.

We set one fair price and stand behind it for five years.

No one needs to recognise the name on your wrist.
