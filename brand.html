00 · The idea

The insider's chronograph.

In June 1980 the Le Jour 7203 stood in a dealer catalogue next to a famous chronograph. Same case, same movement, same maker. One cost $354, the Le Jour $300. Whoever bought the Le Jour knew exactly what they were buying.

Collectors gave these watches a nickname that starts with «poor man's». We turn it around: the buyers were not poorer, they were better informed. Le Jour builds watches for people who know what is inside a watch. So Le Jour says openly what is inside, where it comes from and what it costs. And builds for the day, not for the display case.

  • Knowing. We talk to people who understand watches or want to. We explain without lecturing.
  • Candour. Movement, origin and price logic are in plain sight. What we cannot prove, we do not claim.
  • Fair value. One price, no discounts, everything included.
  • For the day. Watches are worn. At the wheel, on the water, at the desk.

05 · Wordmark

Drawn, not typed. The O is a bezel, the red index is the zero mark.

Ink on light
Cream on dark
  • Construction. Capitals with a constant stroke, 14 units on 100 of cap height, straight ends, curves from pure circle arcs, wide proportions.
  • The O. A perfect circle, like a bezel. At twelve a red index cuts through the ring, rises above the cap height and reaches into the dial: the chronograph hand at rest.
  • Clear space. The height of the O on every side. In tight layouts at least half of it.
  • Minimum size. 96 px wide on screen, 22 mm in print. Below that, the signet alone.

10 · Five principles

Five rules from the old dials carry the whole identity.

  1. 01

    The zero mark.

    A red index at twelve, like a chronograph hand at rest. It sits in the O of the wordmark, it is the signet and the favicon, and on the site it marks the start of everything: a scale, a line, the active state.

  2. 02

    The red hand.

    On the old chronographs exactly one hand was red, the one that moved. Red belongs only to what moves or acts: the primary button, the running seconds hand, progress, focus. Never decoration, never a discount, at most one red element per screen.

  3. 03

    The six o'clock line.

    Old dials said below the six where they came from. At the six o'clock position of every Le Jour surface stands a plain line: movement, size, water resistance, and once proven, assembly and final inspection. What stands there is true.

  4. 04

    Panda contrast.

    Two surfaces, Panda Black and Dial Cream. Pages change between them like panda and reverse panda. Colour comes only from the dials.

  5. 05

    Minute markers.

    Sections are numbered like a minute track: 00, 05, 10, 15. Dividers are minute scales. The site reads like a dial.

25 · Grid

Built on a dial.

  • 12, 8, 4 columns. Margins 48, 32, 16 px. Gutters 24, 20, 16 px. Content up to 1'440 px.
  • Spacing on an 8 scale. 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128. Sections breathe 128 px on desktop, 72 px on a phone.
  • Corners. Zero on surfaces, cards and images, for instrument precision. Two pixels on buttons and fields. Circles only for round things: swatches, totalisators, the signet.
  • The dial grid. Big compositions align to 12, 3, 6 and 9 of an imagined circle. Headline at nine, watch at three, six o'clock line at the bottom centre.

30 · Motion

Like an instrument. Ticking, not floating.

  • Precise. No spring, no bounce, no wobble.
  • Eight beats a second. Seconds hands jump eight times a second, as the SW200 and SW500 do at 28'800 vph. Collectors notice.
  • Scrolling measures. Progress shows as a chronograph counter, not a bar.
  • Calm comes first. With reduced motion: only fades, hands rest at ten past ten.

45 · Tone of voice

Quiet certainty.

One insider showing another something. Short, precise, warm. One dry line of humour per page. Facts as feeling, not as a data sheet.

We write

  • 42 millimetres. Fits under a shirt cuff. Just.
  • Sellita SW500. 28'800 beats an hour.
  • Start the chronograph at the first kilometre marker.
  • 200 metres. Enough for the lake, the sea and most of what you'll do.
  • A dealer catalogue from June 1980 lists the 7203 at $300.
  • Delivered duty paid. The price you see is the price you pay.

We never write

  • Perfect size for every wrist
  • Premium movement
  • Designed for the modern adventurer
  • Unbreakable, the best
  • Heritage since the 1960s
  • Limited time offer

Words we leave out

luxury · timeless · iconic · elevate · crafted with passion · legendary · ultimate · must-have · limited time · exclusive offer · journey · unleash · and any claim of Swiss origin until it is proven per line

50 · Imagery

Hard light, real wrists, real places.

  • Light. One hard key light, low and from the side, like late afternoon. Deep shadows, warm highlights.
  • Material. Brushed against polished steel, reflections in domed sapphire, the texture of the dial.
  • Wrist. Hands that do something: a steering wheel, a sheet, a coffee cup. Faces cropped or turned away. Never posed.
  • Places. Swiss pass roads at sunrise, a regatta on the lake, a workbench, an early train, a late table. No postcards.

Mood images for the first shoot. The place photographs are generated references for light and mood, not campaign images.
